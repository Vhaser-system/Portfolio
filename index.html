AI-Augmented · Human-Reviewed

These documents were generated by AI under my direction and reviewed by me — not written by me, and not peer-reviewed scholarship. I publish them to show how I calibrate the tool: each report is instructed to label its own evidence strength, attach numeric confidence estimates, surface alternative interpretations, audit its own biases, and list concrete steps a reader can use to verify it.

The aim was breadth of exploration for a story world, so the sourcing spans academic work, industry writing, and public forums. Read these as a calibrated research draft — a demonstration of directed, self-critical AI, not a citation of record. The intent is a map toward verifiable information, not a final answer, which is why each report is built to be checked rather than trusted on sight.

AI Animation Pipeline — From Platform to Product

A self-directed build: an AI-driven flat 2D animation pipeline, taken from an ambitious general-purpose platform down to a narrow, demoable product. I'm a writer, not a trained engineer — AI wrote the majority of the code; my work was specifying it, reviewing what came back, catching errors before they compounded, and knowing when to stop. It shipped as a public, test-covered repository. I then deliberately wound down the broad version once it was clear it had no defensible market. The documents below cover what I built, where I misjudged the scope, and why stopping was the right call.

Undergraduate Capstone Quantitative Research Python · Regression & PCA 2022–2023

Does a User's Age Predict Their Avatar? — Graduation Thesis

My Creative Business graduation thesis at Breda University of Applied Sciences: a solo, mixed-method study testing whether the characteristics of a self-created avatar correlate with a user's age — the kind of signal a platform would need before it could profile or predict behaviour. I built the method on Personal Construct Psychology and the repertory-grid technique, ran 12 qualitative interviews to derive a shared rating framework, then ran two surveys — one collecting age and avatars (231 responses, 212 usable age/avatar pairs) and a separate one gathering 841 avatar-rating responses — and analysed the data in Python with linear regression and Principal Component Analysis.

The hypothesis was not confirmed: I found no significant correlation between age and avatar characteristics — age alone is a weak basis for profiling. The honest null result is the point, and the study additionally validated the repertory grid as a low-noise way to measure how people perceive avatars. It ran entirely on statistical methods in Python, with no generative AI — it predates and stands apart from my later AI-augmented work.
